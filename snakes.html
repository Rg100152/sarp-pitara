First Aid: Do's & Don'ts

RIGHT PROTOCOL · GOLDEN HOUR
LIFE-SAVING

✓ DO — Right Protocol

  • Reassure the patient and keep them calm and lying down. Panic speeds venom circulation.
  • Immobilise the bitten limb with a splint or loose crepe bandage — like a fracture. Keep it still.
  • Remove rings, watches, bangles, anklets and tight shoes before swelling starts.
  • Clean the wound gently with clean water. Do not scrub.
  • Note the time of the bite and watch for ptosis, breathing difficulty or bleeding.
  • Call 108 and move to the nearest PHC / CHC / district hospital with ASV.
  • Remember RIGHT: Reassure · Immobilise · Get to hospital · Hospital treatment · Tell symptoms.

✕ DON'T — Fatal Mistakes

  • Never tie a tight tourniquet or rope — it causes gangrene and limb loss.
  • Never cut, slash or burn the wound.
  • Never try to suck out venom with your mouth.
  • Never apply ice, acid, herbs, cow dung or "snake stones".
  • Never give tea, coffee, alcohol or painkillers like aspirin/ibuprofen.
  • Never waste time catching or killing the snake.
  • Never visit an ojha / tantrik. The only antidote is ASV in a hospital.

Myth-Buster: Why Superstition Kills

THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE ILLUSION
AWARENESS

70–80% of bites are non-venomous. Rat snakes, blind snakes and water snakes inject no venom. A patient survives regardless of the ojha — but the ojha takes the credit.

20–30% of venomous bites are dry bites. No venom is injected. The patient survives, reinforcing the superstition.

Real envenomation is different. When a krait, cobra or viper injects venom, delay of 2–3 hours can mean respiratory failure or fatal bleeding before the patient reaches hospital.

✕
Neem leaf chewing does not reveal venom. Taste has no relation to neurotoxins or hemotoxins.
✕
Snake stones do not absorb venom. Clinical trials show no effect.
✕
Chicken / pigeon "suction" is unscientific and causes dangerous wound infection.
✓
Science saves: ASV, 20WBCT, airway support and ICU care. Reach a hospital within the golden hour.
